# Facilitator guide

Status: **Draft for the professor's review.** It turns the training checklist in
[CONSENT_AND_DATA_GOVERNANCE.md](../CONSENT_AND_DATA_GOVERNANCE.md) §7 into a session plan
and a field card. Before the pilot it must be checked against the ethics approval (Q-L3), the
field consent order (Q-G4) and the final consent form, and translated into Hindi.

A facilitator is a trained person who helps speakers contribute, usually on the facilitator's
own phone: elders, people who do not read, or people without a smartphone. Facilitators carry
the most responsibility in Lokdhara, because the speaker trusts them to explain honestly what
happens to their voice.

## 1. Training session (about 2 hours)

| Part | Time | What happens |
|------|------|--------------|
| Why Lokdhara exists | 10 min | Preserving Mundari; who runs the project; where recordings are kept. |
| Consent, by role-play | 40 min | Each trainee explains the consent form to another trainee, in Mundari or Hindi, including saying "no" to some questions. The trainer stops anything that sounds like pressure. |
| The app, hands-on | 40 min | Each trainee, on their own phone with a **test** account: create a speaker, record oral consent, make one recording in each mode (free, prompted, read), add a text, find it under "My submissions", and send a draft after switching mobile data off and on. |
| Hard cases | 20 min | Go through §4 together. |
| Device care and questions | 10 min | §5, then the field card (§6) is handed out. |

A trainee facilitates real speakers only after doing the hands-on part without help. The
trainer records who was trained and when (outside the app; no personal data in this repository).

## 2. Before each visit

- Phone charged, app opened once while online (it then keeps working without signal).
- Signed in with your **own** account. Never share your account or let a speaker use it alone.
- Pick a quiet place: no TV, fan or road noise if possible. Other people's voices in the
  background make a recording unusable for sharing.
- Have the printed consent explanation in Mundari and Hindi, and the withdrawal contact card.

## 3. With each speaker

1. **Introduce yourself and the project** in the speaker's preferred language. Say clearly
   that taking part is voluntary and that saying no changes nothing for them.
2. **Create the speaker** in the app (speaker code only; name and phone go in the restricted
   identity section, which only admins can see). Confirm the speaker is **18 or older**; the
   pilot records no minors (Q-L4).
3. **Consent before recording.** Read or play the consent form. Ask each question separately
   and accept each answer as given, including "no" or "I don't know" (treat that as no). Record
   the consent exchange in the app (oral consent) or note the paper form number if the
   institution uses paper.
4. **Record.** Choose the mode that suits the speaker: free speech, a picture or situation
   prompt, or reading a sentence. Short recordings are fine. Stop if the speaker is tired or
   uncomfortable.
5. **Listen for problems** before sending: other identifiable voices, names, phone numbers,
   addresses or things the speaker may later regret sharing. If unsure, ask the speaker whether
   to keep it, and delete the draft if they hesitate.
6. **Send** when you have signal. Drafts stay on the phone until they are sent; the app retries
   by itself. Check "My submissions" shows the recording as received.
7. **Before leaving**, tell the speaker how to change their mind later (§4) and leave the
   withdrawal contact card.

## 4. Hard cases

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|-----------|
| The speaker wants to stop or take back something | Stop at once. In the app, open Withdrawals and request one (all their contributions, or specific ones), or tell an admin the same day. Never argue or ask why. |
| A family member answers consent for the speaker | Only the speaker can consent. If the speaker cannot understand the form, do not record. |
| Someone under 18 wants to take part | Thank them; the pilot does not record minors. |
| Ritual, sacred or private knowledge comes up | Ask whether it may be shared. If in doubt, do not record it, and tell the project lead so the community advisers can decide (Q-G3). |
| Someone else is clearly audible | Re-record in a quieter moment. That person would have to consent separately. |
| The app shows an error or the phone dies | Drafts are kept on the phone. Try again later with signal. If a draft is lost, record again only if the speaker agrees. |
| Someone asks for payment or promises | Follow the project's agreed answer (to be set by the professor). Do not make promises yourself. |

## 5. Device care

- Lock your phone with a PIN. If it is lost or stolen, tell an admin the same day so your
  sessions can be signed out.
- Don't copy recordings to WhatsApp, the gallery or a computer. Recordings made with a separate
  recorder app are uploaded through Lokdhara and then deleted from the phone.
- Sign out on shared phones.

## 6. Field card (print, one page)

1. Voluntary, and "no" is always fine.
2. 18 or older only.
3. Consent first, question by question, in the speaker's language.
4. Quiet place; no other voices; no names, numbers or addresses.
5. Speaker wants to stop or withdraw → stop, request withdrawal, tell an admin today.
6. Send drafts when you have signal; check "My submissions".
7. Phone locked; no copies anywhere else.
8. Problems: _project lead's contact (from the contact sheet)_.
